Overview

The problem

At Yowza's physical stores, loyalty ran on paper stamp cards. Customers regularly lost or forgot them, lost track of how close they were to a free dessert, and rarely heard about promotions until they were already at the counter. Paying and topping up was manual and slow. The result was a loyalty programme that quietly leaked the very engagement it was meant to build.

The goal

Replace the paper card with one playful, on-brand app that brings rewards, payments, and promotions into a single place — so customers can track stamps, redeem treats, top up a wallet, and discover offers without friction.

What the app needed to deliver

Understanding the user

Research approach

I ran 20 interviews with regular Yowza customers and spent time observing behaviour in-store — watching how people tracked stamps, asked staff about rewards, and paid at the counter. A 20-person survey then helped me measure how widely those frustrations were felt. The recurring theme was clear: the paper loyalty card was doing more harm than good.

82%
prefer digital loyalty over paper cards
76%
would visit more if rewards auto-tracked
71%
want a mobile wallet for quick pay
84%
say an app makes Yowza feel modern

What surprised me

I expected the headline ask to be faster payment. It mattered — but the deeper frustration was emotional. Losing a paper card didn't just cost a free dessert; it felt like throwing away progress people had earned visit by visit. That reframed the brief: the rewards tracker wasn't a nice-to-have, it was the heart of the product.

Survey deep-dive

The survey confirmed which frustrations were most widely felt — and pointed straight at the rewards tracker as the feature to get right first.

Usability study

I tested the high-fidelity prototype with 5 regular customers on four core tasks — register & log in, check rewards, top up and pay, and find and redeem an offer.

1

Digital beat paper instantly. 4 of 5 users understood the rewards system right away and called it far more convenient than a stamp card.

2

Top Up was hard to spot. Several users missed the button at first, so I increased its visibility and moved it to a clearer position.

3

All-in-one felt “clean & modern”. Combining wallet and rewards was the most praised idea — it made the app feel current and trustworthy.

4

Onboarding needed signposting. Minor first-run confusion led me to add guided tooltips that introduce scanning, rewards and the wallet.

★

What I learned

Research earns you the right to design.

The strongest decisions in this project came straight from listening. Talking to customers who'd lost their cards reframed the whole brief — payment speed was real, but the emotional weight of lost progress was bigger. I learned how much clear navigation and information architecture carry usability, how a single misplaced button can derail a task, and how much a tight feedback-and-iteration loop sharpens the end experience. Most of all, I practised balancing what users need with what the business — more visits, more loyalty — needs too.

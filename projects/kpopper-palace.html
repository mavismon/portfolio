01

Overview

The problem

Almost every K-pop store in Myanmar runs entirely on Facebook. To pre-order, a fan has to message the page admin, wait for a reply, transfer money, and hope nothing goes wrong. In practice it often does: items sell out after payment, order numbers get forgotten, replies arrive hours late, and there's no way to track a delivery or trust a seller.

The goal

Give K-pop fans a dedicated mobile app where they can confidently order pre-order and in-stock items from anywhere, in minutes — with reviews, clear product details, order tracking, and a fairer way to get the version they actually want.

My role

UX/UI designer in a three-person team (Team-A) during a UX/UI course at Acodemy, Yangon. I contributed across research, IA, wireframing, and the high-fidelity prototype.

Project at a glance

Kpoper Palace is a one-stop mobile shop for K-pop fans — albums, lightsticks, season's greetings and more — with a playful design and all the shopping features fans actually need.

02

Understanding the user

Market research

Around 99.99% of K-pop stores in Myanmar sell entirely through Facebook pages. When a pre-order opens, the admin posts details and prices, and buyers message the chatbox to order. On paper it looks quick — in reality it breaks down constantly. One buyer messaged at 12:00 P.M. and the admin replied at 11:19 P.M. That eleven-hour gap, repeated across every order, is exactly the friction the app set out to remove.

Competitive analysis

With almost no local apps to study, we looked at two established international K-pop stores to learn what works and what to avoid.

03

Starting the design

Information architecture

We grouped everything fans do into five tabs — Home, Category, Code, Wallet, Profile — so browsing, ordering, and managing a collection all stay within reach.

Core user flow — pre-ordering an album
Browse / search→ Product details→ Read reviews→ Add to cart→ Pay by card→ Track order
Wireframes

I created 12 low-fidelity wireframes to lock structure and flow before any visual design — the storefront, product detail, cart and profile — testing that fans could pre-order in a few taps.

What I learned

Research surfaces the feature you'd never guess.

As my first end-to-end project — and my first time designing in a team — Kpoper Palace taught me how much research drives good design. None of us would have prioritised a trade-in service from intuition alone; it came straight from listening to fans. I learned how clear information architecture makes a content-heavy store usable, how reviews and tracking build trust in a market that runs on it, and how to split work and keep one coherent experience across three designers.

NexCell Solutions and Propily

My internship at NexCell Solutions covered two pieces of work. I redesigned the company website, and I worked on the admin CRM for Propily, the AI CRM for UK estate agencies that NexCell builds. I worked as a user experience designer and product UI designer across both.

The company and the product

NexCell Solutions builds custom AI solutions and software for businesses, and is moving from consultancy work towards its own products. Propily is its flagship: an AI CRM and operating system for UK estate and letting agencies. It brings enquiries, leads, viewings, tenancies, compliance and finance into one system, with an in-app AI assistant. It was previously called ConneX.

Part 1: NexCell website redesign

The brief

NexCell wanted its website to move from a consultancy page to an enterprise-grade product company site, in a new blue brand. The first review of the live homepage found positioning, trust claims and product links that needed fixing before the rebuild.

Audit and direction

  • Audited the live site and ranked every issue by impact: positioning, trust claims, product links, images, contact details and the AI summary links.
  • Flagged unverifiable claims and testimonials as a legal risk under UK consumer law, and listed each one to be confirmed or removed.
  • Researched inspiration sites and wrote a section map for the homepage, from the hero and products through to proof, team, security, careers and contact.

Site map and copy

  • Mapped the full site: 17 pages across the company pages, six service pages and the legal pages.
  • Wrote a copy table that shows the status of every item, so the team could see what was final, what was a draft and what was still missing.
  • Grouped the services into three clear blocks with one product link, instead of one long list.

Design

  • Designed every page for desktop and mobile in Figma, with shared header and footer components and a mobile menu.
  • Set up a style guide covering colour, typography, spacing, border radius and elevation, with a components page for reuse.
  • Designed the Propily page as its own explainer, with a link out to the product site.

Feedback and final designs

  • Took feedback on SEO, motion, the depth of the service pages and the Propily page.
  • Produced final homepage designs for desktop and mobile, including modal and hover states for the components.

Part 2: Propily CRM UX and product UI

My role

I work as a user experience designer and product UI designer on the CRM. I take each admin page through the same steps: audit it, redesign it, then check the live version with the developers who build it.

Audit

  • Walked through the product as an agent would, noting every screen worth showing and how each one is used.
  • Audited nine admin pages: Today, Inbox, Tasks, Calendar, Leads, Lead capture, Contacts, Matching and Deals. Checked contrast, layout and form friction by eye.

Redesign

  • Drew a Before and After design in Figma for each of the nine pages. Each After has a spec panel with numbered notes for the developers.
  • Prioritised the findings into fix first, next and polish. The main theme was trust: the same figure showing different values on different pages matters more than visual polish.

Cross-check

  • Checked the live leads, contacts and deals pages: working controls, correct data, empty and error states, responsive layout and brand consistency.
  • Logged each issue as a blocker, major or minor, with what I did, what I expected and what happened.
  • Continuing the UI improvements one section at a time, as set out in the weekly brief.

What the two have in common

Both pieces came back to one question: can the reader believe what they see? On the website, every claim had to be provable. In the CRM, every number had to agree across pages.

A company site has to earn trust before it earns a click, and a CRM is only useful if people believe the numbers on screen.

Status: both are in progress. The website is waiting on final content and approvals, and the CRM work continues section by section.
